Claude Design brief — D1: Moments Studio's admin surface, the Generated kind in the reader, YouTube's privacy notice and page, and the motion budget's extension

Run this against the **Fergie Time** design system project. Written by Kickoff's PM seat (Claude Code) on Thu 8 Oct 2026 (TZ=America/New_York) from a fresh read of `main` at `54f7804`; every value below was read from a file or a measured receipt in that session, and anything invented for illustration is labelled ILLUSTRATIVE. Precedence for this cycle: **the Fergie Time design system > this brief > any implementation prompt written from your canvas.** Where this brief and the design system disagree, the design system wins and you say so on the canvas.

WHAT KICKOFF IS. A Big-5 European football fixtures and standings tracker in Brooklyn time, a static React app on GitHub Pages (`https://benicheni.github.io/kickoff/`, repo `https://github.com/BeniCheni/kickoff`, v0.5.2). Its promise is honest time and honest data: it never renders a kickoff the league has not set and never presents an invented number as real. Three lenses (Ledger, Poster, Broadcast) × two themes render the same data at different loudness. `src/index.css` and `src/lib/competitions.ts` are the token source of truth; the design system mirrors them.

WHAT MOMENTS IS NOW. A third tab, a gallery of short football moments played in the page. Its design came from two earlier rounds Beni approved on 25 Sep 2026: **Matchnight** (the gallery, with original typographic covers VOICES, SEVEN and TOGETHER, a "Your evening" queue, Previous/Next, Save reference, spoiler-light mode and a "Find your next moment" filter) and **Floodlights Cinema** (an overlay that enlarges the player beside the queue). Those rounds' files are not in the repo; the design is recorded in `docs/moments-architecture.md`, and Fergie Time has no Moments components yet, so this cycle is also the first time Moments enters the design system. Today the tab is inert: the published edition is empty, so readers see no player. Two halves will fill it. (1) Curated YouTube highlights from rights holders' official channels, played through YouTube's embedded player; their first edition is close (slice 4, `docs/moments-slice-4-spec.md`; source rules in `docs/moments-content-policy.md`). (2) Short clips of two fictional footballers, Beni's cats Fenway and Frankenstein, generated through fal by a local admin tool on Beni's Mac called Moments Studio, exported to the edition by pull request, and played as plain MP4 in the same player host (`docs/moments-studio-architecture-review.md`).

THIS BRIEF COVERS FOUR GROUPS, AND NOTHING ELSE.

A. The Generated kind in the reader (Studio slices R1 and R2 build from your canvas).
A1. The Generated badge. It renders from the record's kind, in page text and not only in pixels, on the gallery card, on the stage and in Cinema, and no setting, filter or spoiler-light mode hides it (review §11.6 item 1). It also has to work as the lighter disclosure EU AI Act Article 50(4) asks of evidently fictional work, if that applies. Note the colour rule: floodlight amber means "check this number's provenance" and nothing else; decide whether a kind label is that, and say why.
A2. Hosted-player chrome. A plain `<video>` inside the same host as the YouTube frame, never moved, shown when a hosted item plays and hidden otherwise. No request before Play: no `src`, no poster from another origin, `preload="none"` until Play (rule K3). Native controls or Kickoff's own Play/Pause (the YouTube items use Kickoff's own Play button): your call, and say what keyboard and focus do. A same-origin poster is open: it costs repo weight and is your decision (review §13, deferred row).
A3. Sound. Every generated clip so far carries a generated AAC stereo soundtrack (measured); Play is a user gesture, so sound on Play is allowed. What the design does with an unwanted soundtrack (start muted with a visible sound state, or with sound, or a per-item authored choice) is yours.
A4. The frame ratio (ideas row 105). The stage anchor and the Cinema slot are `aspect-ratio: 16 / 9` with `min-height: 200px` (`src/index.css`), so the box is near 16:10 only at phone widths. The clips generated so far are 768 × 480 (16:10). On Beni's real visit of 7 Oct a 16:10 clip pillarboxed (side bars) in the 16:9 box at 390 and 1000. Options: accept the bars, give the generated kind its own 16:10 frame, or have the Studio generate 16:9 keyframes (H3 takes its canvas from the input image; a 16:9 result is UNVERIFIED). Your recommendation feeds the Studio's keyframe settings.
A5. Kind-specific recovery copy (collision C9). Today's sentences, verbatim from `src/lib/momentsRecovery.ts`, are written for YouTube: blocked — heading "Keep the story. Choose the next step.", body "The owner does not allow playback on other websites. … The official source is also available. External playback is unverified."; timeout — "Playback not confirmed." / "Something has not connected. The cause is unknown. This does not establish a territory restriction. …"; unavailable — "This selection cannot be played here." / "The provider reported that this upload is unavailable. … The official source is also available. External playback is unverified." "Owner" and "official source" are false for a hosted clip, which has no outside page (C3: a generated item shows no outbound link). Write the hosted sentences (network error, decode error, a file that will not load) in the same voice, and keep "Next opens "<title>"." where it applies.
A6. Provenance for a generated item. Beni ruled (15.7) that the generation prompt is published with the clip. Design where it and the rest of the provenance read: the model endpoint and the date it was pinned, when it was generated, the fictional-players attestation, the parent generation if it is a regeneration. Facts, not marketing; the existing "Source & curation" disclosure is a candidate home.
A7. Order and filters. Generated items have no fixture and no kickoff. Beni ruled (15.6) that in Newest they keep authored order after the dated fixtures. They get their own category (studio), and the badge must not become something a filter removes.

B. YouTube publication duties (slice 4 S7 builds from your canvas). Beni ruled on 7 Oct 2026 that the first published YouTube edition ships with a privacy page and a notice at Play. The primary sources, read 7 Oct: YouTube's API Services Developer Policies (last updated 14 Sep 2026) require that each site using the API "require users to agree to a privacy policy before users can access" its features, that the policy link Google's Privacy Policy (http://www.google.com/policies/privacy) and say the site uses YouTube API Services (III.A.2), and that a page showing YouTube content make clear YouTube is the source (III.F.2.a). Required Minimum Functionality: the player at least 200 × 200 px, nothing drawn in front of any part of it, no autoplay. Kickoff already meets the last three; your designs must keep meeting them.
B1. The notice at first Play: "Plays on YouTube · Privacy" is Beni's working text, not final. Placement beside the Play control on the stage and in Cinema, at 360, 390 and 1000; whether it shows before every first Play of a visit or only once (remembering it would need a new browser-storage key, which the privacy page would then have to list); the "agree" mechanism, made honest and light. Beni may ask counsel to check the wording; design so wording can change without layout change.
B2. The privacy page. Write it from these facts, all read from the code on 8 Oct: the app keeps three browser-storage keys and no cookies — `kickoff-theme` and `kickoff-theme-broadcast` (theme choices) and `kickoff-moments-saved-v1` (saved Moment references); there is no analytics, beacon or tracking code; every page load fetches fonts from Google Fonts (`fonts.googleapis.com`, `fonts.gstatic.com`), so Google sees each visit; the YouTube player loads nothing until the reader presses Play (verified on Beni's 7 Oct visit), and after Play YouTube and Google may set cookies and serve ads; clearing site data in the browser removes everything Kickoff stores. When hosted clips arrive, the media host (not yet chosen; Beni rules at slice ST4) will also see each play, and the page must grow a line for it (review §13, PUB row). Where the page lives is your proposal, under one constraint: the `?only=` and `&date=` URL parameters are a contract another system reads, and must not change.
B3. YouTube as the source. Check that every YouTube item's card and stage name the source channel and link the watch page, alongside the player's own branding.

C. The Studio admin surface (Studio slice ST3 builds from your canvas). A local web page served on Beni's Mac on the loopback address only, for one operator; desktop first (judge at ~1280 and 1000), same tokens and type as the reader. What it does: compose a generation (recipe v0 is keyframes: three stills K0, K1, K2 from Beni's photos, then one image-to-video request; the model comes from a reviewed registry, never typed), show the dollar estimate before submit against Beni's caps ($1.50 per job, where a job is one fal request; $6 per session; the fal balance kept at or below $9.37, topped up by hand only), track each job, review the result, keep or discard, regenerate (a new generation with the old one as parent), and export kept clips as a draft pull request (the media upload is asked for per export). The job states, all of which need a designed state (review §8.2): draft · budget-refused (never submitted) · submitted · queued · in-progress · completed · downloaded · kept · discarded · failed (an HTTP error or error payload) · refused (the model's safety checker; never retried automatically and never shown as a failure of the tool; it can arrive after two minutes) · timed-out · cancelled. Two Studio-level states: **database-unavailable** (the Studio's records live in a Supabase Postgres project, which pauses after a week idle on the free plan; in this state every paid submit is refused because the estimate and the ledger line must be written first, drafts and the local cache still work, and restoring is one click in Supabase's dashboard, which the page should say) and **out of credit** (fal answers "User is locked. Reason: Exhausted balance"; a state, not an error). Measured costs to use as real sample data (Appendix S, 4–5 Oct): a 15 s 480P clip on minimax/h3-max-turbo $0.225 (rising to $0.375 after 15 Oct); K0 from four photos $0.051; one H3-only clip end to end $0.30 to $0.37 now, $0.45 to $0.52 after 15 Oct; a 15 s clip is about 10 MB. The cost meter shows the estimate before and the billed amount after (fal reports billable units in a response header). The per-clip review checklist the Confirm step walks (review §11.6 item 7): extra players or officials; text on boards, shirts or screens; logo-like or crest-like marks on kit, ball or boards; the subject's identity and posture; the soundtrack — every model in the spike added marks and text nobody asked for, and a prompt cannot stop it. The attestation records that the checklist was walked, by whom and when. Beni's photographs never leave his Mac except as stripped uploads; nothing on the page shows a photo's location.

D. The motion budget's extension. Fergie Time's budget is complete: 150 ms ease (row expansion, tab underline), 180 ms cubic-bezier(.2,.7,.2,1) (lens thumb), 220 ms ease (lens cross-fade), plus the marquee; "Nothing else animates"; reduced motion collapses to opacity. Beni ruled (15.10) that motion stays zero-dependency: CSS, the Web Animations API and, behind a feature test with a fallback, view transitions. Any new motion — Cinema entering and leaving, a job's progress, a badge, a hover on the video — is a design-system decision this cycle makes or declines. Deliver a motion spec: for each motion, the trigger, duration, easing, the reduced-motion form and the platform API; or "no new motion" with the reason. If something cannot be done on the platform, name it; the first library to weigh would be `motion/mini` (about 3 kB gzip), and only with a written exception. The video-library question is settled: plain MP4, no hls.js (ruled 15.5).

Matchnight and Floodlights Cinema as approved (with the stage stacking at 887 px and below, side by side from 888, and the 780 px header); the three covers; the one player host that is never moved or remounted; no autoplay on entry or re-entry; the 200 px floor; Play as the only way playback starts; the three lenses and two themes; hard rules 1–6 (never a kickoff time the league has not set; never an invented number as real; amber for data trust only; two clocks from one instant; no motion, gradient or accent beyond the system's; the Table screen is settled); the icon vocabulary (emoji and unicode glyphs, no icon set); the `?only=` and `&date=` URL contract; Fixtures and Table are untouched. Beni's Studio rulings of 5 Oct (15.1–15.14) and his 7 Oct source rulings (official channels only, 90-day permissions) are context, not design questions.
